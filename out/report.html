Cross-venue derivatives research · BTC & ETH

The weekend nobody hedges

Kalshi, Polymarket and Deribit agree on Bitcoin and Ether to within a few basis points on every day that Deribit lists an option. They diverge sharply on exactly the two days it doesn't — and that gap is the only thing in this dataset that survives fees, spreads and a bootstrap.

What was pulled

Three venues, complete books, one simultaneous snapshot. Kalshi and Polymarket each list two redundant ladders per event — a "price above X" digital ladder and a "price in bucket [a,b)" ladder — that settle on the identical print, so each venue states its distribution twice. Deribit supplies the reference options surface.

The chain

Four things had to be ruled out first

01

Static arbitrage inside each venue — none

Each event's two ladders were thrown into one linear program over the exact state partition: maximise the worst-case payoff of a prepaid portfolio subject to book depth. A positive optimum is money regardless of where the underlying prints. Across all 176 events the best result was 6 bp. These books are internally clean.

02

Level arbitrage between venues — none, once the peg is applied

Polymarket settles on Binance USDT; Kalshi and Deribit on USD. Binance's BTC/USDC price sits on the USD composite, so the whole gap is the stablecoin peg — currently for BTC. Apply it and the two venues' implied forwards agree to $3 on a $63,650 underlying. Skip it and you invent a 2-point probability edge on every at-the-money digital.

03

Shape edge versus Deribit — vanishes under execution

Ladders looked 1–3 vol points rich. Almost all of it was a stale-price artifact: pricing against an external spot instead of each ladder's own implied forward turns a $54 snapshot-timing gap into a 0.6σ "edge" on a contract 24 minutes from expiry. Re-centred on each ladder's own forward and required to survive a ±2 vol-point shift, the surviving edges are pennies.

04

The clock is not flat — and this is where it opens up

Deribit's dailies expire 08:00Z. Polymarket settles 16:00Z. Kalshi settles 16:00Z and 21:00Z. Comparing them requires knowing how variance actually accrues through the day, and it does not accrue evenly.

The finding

Saturday realises a quarter of a weekday

The intraday profile has a much larger sibling. Measuring the exact window each contract settles on — 16:00Z on day D−1 to 16:00Z on day D — over two years gives 104 independent observations per weekday, with no model in between.

Nobody lists a weekend expiry

Deribit jumps from Friday 7 August straight to Friday 14 August. Kalshi's listings stop at Aug 5 00:00Z and Aug 7 21:00Z. Polymarket lists every day, including Saturday Aug 8 and Sunday Aug 9.

So the weekend contracts are the only instruments in the entire three-venue universe exposed to Saturday and Sunday variance, and there is no listed options market anywhere that can discipline them. That is precisely the condition under which a mispricing survives.

Construction

Deribit sets the level, history sets only the weekend shape

Each source is used only where it is strong. Total implied variance out to Deribit's last listed expiry — Friday 7 Aug 08:00Z, a liquid arbitraged point — is taken as given. It is then extended across the weekend using the directly measured variance of those exact clock windows, rescaled so a midweek window matches what Deribit itself charges.

No assumption about the variance risk premium is required: the level comes from implied, not realised. The rescaling factor is 0.66× for BTC and 0.52× for ETH — Deribit currently implies well below the two-year realised average, consistent with the calm 30-day regime.

Two cross-checks matter. First, on every day Deribit does list an expiry, Polymarket's total implied variance runs 1.09–1.29× Deribit's — an ordinary retail premium, stable across six ladders and both assets. Second, the distortion is concentrated in the increments, not the totals: Polymarket's Aug-9 total variance is only 1.05× (BTC) and 1.28× (ETH) fair, but it gets there by charging almost nothing for Saturday and roughly double for Sunday. The venue has close to the right amount of variance for the weekend as a whole and has distributed it across the two days almost backwards.

The trade — and its binding constraint

The edge is real; taking it at market is not

Short the Sat→Sun forward variance: sell the Aug 9 ladder's variance and buy the Aug 8 ladder's, in the ratio that leaves the position vega-neutral to everything before Saturday 16:00Z. On a complete bucket ladder that is a weighted strip — buy bucket i with weight ln(K_i/F)² — whose expected payoff is exactly the contract's total variance.

On BTC both legs earn: the Saturday increment is being given away at 0.15× fair while the Sunday increment is sold at 1.97×, so long-Aug-8 / short-Aug-9 collects on each side rather than paying away one to fund the other. On ETH the same short-Sunday leg pairs against a cheap Friday (0.49×) instead.

Then the books bite. The BTC Aug-9 variance strip costs 0.0051 to buy and fetches 0.0022 to sell — a 40% half-spread on the very object being traded. ETH's weekend buckets quote 6–8¢ wide on values of 20–40¢. A 2.0–2.5× variance mispricing worth ~4¢ on the body bucket does not clear that in a single crossing trade.

Which dictates the implementation. Polymarket charges no maker fee, so the edge is harvested by posting, not crossing: resting bids on the Aug-9 body buckets and offers on its wings, inside a 6–8¢ touch, on the side the model favours. Filled at mid, the ETH Aug-9 1,800–1,900 bucket is worth 0.409 against a 0.330 / 0.410 market — roughly 4¢ of edge on ~3,000 contracts of resting size per bucket. The same structure re-lists every single week.

What would break it

  • Regime, not shape. The level is anchored on Deribit, but the weekend shape is historical. A scheduled weekend event — an exchange failure, a governance vote, a macro shock landing Friday night — is exactly what the historical average cannot see.
  • Thin books cut both ways. $2,790 of lifetime volume on the BTC Aug-9 range ladder means fills are slow and a resting order can sit through a real move.
  • Fit noise. Forward variance is a difference of two fitted quantities on coarse 2,000-wide ladders. The bootstrap resamples each quote inside its own bid/ask and the signal survives, but the point estimates are not precise to the last digit.
  • The peg is the residual, not zero. Any Kalshi-versus-Polymarket leg carries the USDT basis: currently −10.5 bp, two-year mean −9.2 bp, and a 5-day change with a standard deviation of ~2.4 bp (≈ $15 on BTC). Small, mean-reverting, but not nothing.
